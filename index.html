From Passive Filter to Intelligent Outcome Service

Seven perspectives challenge one fixed industrial filtration idea.

MANN+HUMMEL · 24 September 2026 · V2.1
Fixed challenge

How might we turn industrial filtration from a passive component into an intelligent service that predicts performance, reduces energy use and guarantees customer outcomes?

SIMULATED EXERCISE The viewpoints, ideas and dialogue are fictional illustrations. They do not represent what the named individuals actually said or necessarily believe.
Step 01 / 05

Challenge Framing

The fixed filtration challenge, operator pains and desired gains.

Job-to-be-Done

When industrial operators rely on filtration-critical processes, they want predictable filtration performance with minimal energy use and maintenance attention, so they can protect uptime, quality and operating cost without constantly managing the filtration system.

Top 3 Pains
  • Reactive, maintenance-heavy filtration.
  • Energy cost is disconnected from customer economics.
  • Customers buy hardware but still carry performance risk.
Top 3 Gains
  • Predictable performance.
  • Lower total energy and maintenance burden.
  • A supplier accountable for measurable outcomes.
Step 02 / 05

Expert Panel

Distinct perspectives selected to challenge the concept.

The panel tests the fixed filtration service idea against environmental impact, whole-site design, circular materials, market discovery, customer economics, reliability and the practical conditions for scale.
Expert 01

Ani Dasgupta

H6 · President & CEO, World Resources Institute

Systems-oriented climate and development leader who views environmental action as inseparable from economic development, urban transformation, equity and human well-being. Connects local interventions to institutional scale, governance, finance, inclusion and implementation capacity.

Bold Idea

Build a verified multi-site service model: define an energy and performance baseline at each plant, then link the filtration KPI to financing, governance and operator outcomes.

Provocation

Who verifies the promised result at every site, and can the operating model scale beyond a pilot?

Expert 02

Kate Orff

H7 · Landscape architect · Founder, SCAPE

Treats landscapes as active infrastructure and favors ecological, adaptive and place-specific solutions that protect people while restoring natural systems. Looks for hybrid blue-green infrastructure, biodiversity, community value and resilience rather than isolated hard-engineering fixes.

Bold Idea

Design a modular filtration service that adapts to each site and minimizes water, material and land impacts alongside energy use and air quality.

Provocation

Will the service improve the whole site system, or simply optimize a filter while shifting impacts elsewhere?

Expert 03

Greta Thunberg

H8 · Climate activist · Fridays for Future

Evaluates proposals through measurable climate impact, ecological limits and intergenerational responsibility rather than corporate narratives or incremental improvement. Looks for real emissions reduction, speed, scientific alignment and evidence of greenwashing or delayed action.

Bold Idea

Set a measurable threshold for net energy and life-cycle emissions reduction before calling the intelligent filtration service a climate solution.

Provocation

What net reduction remains after sensor power, hardware production, maintenance and data processing are counted?

Expert 04

William McDonough

H9 · Architect · Cradle-to-Cradle pioneer

Moves beyond “less bad” sustainability toward regenerative systems where materials are designed for continuous biological or technical cycles. Treats waste, toxicity and disposability as design failures and asks what every material becomes next.

Bold Idea

Make cartridge recovery, safe material cycles and refurbishment part of the service contract so performance gains do not increase waste.

Provocation

What happens to every spent filter and sensor at the end of its service life?

Expert 05

Julian Simon

H10 · Economist · Counter-Malthusian thinker

Views human ingenuity, knowledge, markets and technological substitution as the ultimate resource capable of turning scarcity into innovation and abundance. Looks for price signals, entrepreneurship, substitution, discovery and unintended consequences of restricting growth.

Bold Idea

Run an open filtration performance challenge in which alternative media, control rules and service models compete on verified customer outcomes.

Provocation

Would an outcome-based market discover better filtration solutions than a prescribed technology stack?

Expert 06

Diana Furchtgott-Roth

H11 · Economist · Free-market policy advocate

Starts from affordability, reliability, resilience and economic competitiveness and is skeptical of mandates that impose large costs without demonstrated benefits. Stress-tests full economic cost, energy prices, industrial competitiveness, consumer choice and dependence on subsidies or regulation.

Bold Idea

Offer a voluntary filtration outcome contract with transparent total cost, uptime commitments and a clear exit option; prove it works without subsidy.

Provocation

Would a customer adopt this contract voluntarily after comparing full cost and operational risk?

Expert 07

Michael Shellenberger

H12 · Author · Environmental commentator

Combines environmental concern with advocacy for nuclear power, energy abundance, technological modernization and economic development. Looks for energy density, reliability, scalability and human welfare while challenging symbolic or high-cost climate policies.

Bold Idea

Use only filtration sensors and control actions that create a measurable gain in energy efficiency or reliability at industrial scale.

Provocation

Does each added digital component earn its place through proven energy or uptime gains?

Step 03 / 05

The Collision Meeting

Illustrative dialogue between the selected perspectives.

GT
Greta Thunberg → responding to Diana Furchtgott-Roth

A lower bill is useful, but the pilot must also demonstrate a net reduction in energy use and life-cycle emissions against the current filtration system.

DF
Diana Furchtgott-Roth → responding to Greta Thunberg

Then state the measurement method and the full cost. Customers will adopt a performance contract only if savings and reliability survive real operating conditions.

KO
Kate Orff → responding to Michael Shellenberger

Optimize the full site: air quality, water, materials and maintenance. A smarter filter that shifts burdens to another part of the system is incomplete.

JS
Julian Simon → responding to Kate Orff and Greta Thunberg

Publish the outcome target and let alternative media, controls and service models compete to meet it at lower cost.

WM
William McDonough → responding to Julian Simon

Include cartridge recovery and material reuse in that target. A service that predicts replacement but discards more material is poorly designed.

AD
Ani Dasgupta → responding to the room

Start with one measurable customer outcome, assign responsibility for the data and contract, then test whether the model scales across different plants.

Step 04 / 05

Collision Combinations

Three service concepts derived from the fixed filtration challenge.

Combination

Circular Performance Contract

Combine McDonough’s material-cycle lens with Simon’s open competition: pay for verified filtration outcomes while rewarding cartridge recovery, reuse and better media alternatives.

Combination

Whole-Site Resilience

Combine Orff’s system view with Shellenberger’s reliability test: use minimal sensing to optimize filtration within the full plant energy, water and uptime system.

Combination

Climate Gain Customers Choose

Combine Thunberg’s net-impact threshold with Furchtgott-Roth’s affordability test: offer a voluntary contract only when verified energy and emissions gains come with a credible total-cost case.

Step 05 / 05

The Final Concept

An outcome-based filtration service and three first experiments.

Final Concept

Verified Filtration Outcomes

A proposed filtration-as-an-outcome service that combines physical filtration expertise, limited sensing and adaptive control. The service would predict performance, reduce energy use and commit to a customer KPI such as uptime, air quality or total operating cost. Any guarantee would be defined against a measured baseline and validated in a pilot before commercial launch.

Why It Is Non-Obvious

The offer joins a physical filter, a verified performance baseline, predictive maintenance, energy optimization and contractual accountability. Its value depends on demonstrating net savings and reliable outcomes at the whole-system level, rather than adding digital features alone.

3 First Experiment Steps · ≤ 6 weeks
1

Interview five target customers and quantify one costly filtration outcome in € / energy / downtime / risk.

2

Build the smallest closed-loop demonstrator linking one sensor signal, one filtration parameter and one customer KPI.

3

Offer three customers a low-risk pilot framed around a guaranteed KPI and record willingness to pay, objections and proof requirements.
